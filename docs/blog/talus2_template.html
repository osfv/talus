The grain on the plains

Talus-1 made convincing mountains and islands. Its plains had a problem you could see: a fine, restless grain on ground that should be calm. The benchmark put numbers on it. Plains scored 3.98 times the real-vs-real noise floor, the worst of six terrain types, with 4.6 times too many tiny pits.

The fix: relative heights

Talus-1 saw every map on one global scale, from sea floor to 1,200 m. A plain spans about 5% of that scale. Tiny errors left over from denoising barely register on a mountain, but on a plain they are as tall as the terrain itself.

x = 2 (h − mean) / relief

Talus-2 learns each map's shape at full scale instead. When you generate, the sampler lifts that shape to the elevation you asked for and stretches it to the relief you asked for. If you leave those open, it borrows them from one of the 16 most similar real training maps. The architecture and speed stay the same. Training took 40 minutes, starting from Talus-1.1.

The scorecard

Scored on 2,000 held-out maps the model never saw. 100 means indistinguishable from real procedural terrain. Same protocol, sampler and seeds for every version, so the differences come from the weights.

The roughness dial barely worked before. Now it follows requests twice as closely.

An honest correction

The quick check during training showed an 87% spectrum gain. The full test run says 53%, from 28x to 13x the noise floor. We quote the test.

Where it still falls short

Mountains and ridges

Now the weakest types, at 1.9x and 2.2x the noise floor. They gave back part of Talus-1.1's fine detail.

The spectrum gap

At 13x the real-vs-real floor, detail across scales remains the largest distance to real terrain.

Placement by design

Mean elevation lands almost exactly because the sampler places each shape there. Relief, slope, water and roughness still come from the model.

One world

Talus only knows our procedural generator. Real elevation data comes later.
