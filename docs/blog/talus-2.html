The fix: relative heights

Talus-1 saw every map on one global scale, from sea floor to 1,200 m. A plain spans about 5% of that scale. Tiny errors left over from denoising barely register on a mountain. On a plain they are as tall as the terrain itself.

Talus-2 learns the shape instead. If you leave elevation and relief open, the sampler borrows them from one of the 16 most similar real training maps. The architecture and speed stay the same. Training took 40 minutes, starting from Talus-1.1.

The scorecard

Scored on 2,000 held-out maps the model never saw. 100 means indistinguishable from real procedural terrain. Same protocol, sampler and seeds for every version, so the differences come from the weights.

What moved

Plains, fixed

The worst terrain type became one of the best. Its excess finest-scale power fell from +3.4 to +0.9 decades.

Pits per 1,000 cells

Real terrain has 8.4. Talus-1 had 14.6. Peaks match too: 11.6 against 11.9.

Roughness error

The roughness control barely worked before. It now follows requests twice as closely.

Still original

Maps closer to the training set than 99% of real held-out maps. Talus-1 had 2.5%.

Honest correction

The quick check during training showed an 87% spectrum gain. The full test says 53%, from 28x to 13x the noise floor. We quote the test.

Where it still falls short

Mountains and ridges

Now the weakest types, at 1.9x and 2.2x the noise floor. They gave back part of Talus-1.1's fine detail.

The spectrum gap

At 13x the real-vs-real floor, detail across scales remains the largest distance to real terrain.

Placement by design

Mean elevation lands almost exactly because the sampler places each shape there. Relief, slope, water and roughness still come from the model.

One world

Talus only knows our procedural generator. Real elevation data comes later.
